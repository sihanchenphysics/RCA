Hidden rotation in a noisy traveling pattern

Two coupled complex amplitudes with non-reciprocal coupling generate a rotating pattern; additive Ornstein–Uhlenbeck noise of tunable strength D hides it in the raw field.

Evolution of the profile u(x, t)

Space–time heatmap, newest time at the top. At large D the traveling structure disappears in the noise.

Rotational-mode plane

Projection x1 = ⟨u, w1⟩, x2 = ⟨u, w2⟩ on two rotational modes w1, w2: combinations of cos/sin(kx) and cos/sin(2kx) spanning the plane of the noise-free limit cycle. Color and white arrows: the steady-state probability density p(x) and probability flux J(x), calculated exactly rather than estimated from the simulation. With c(t) the noise-free limit cycle in the plane (period T) and GD the 2-d Gaussian of variance D per axis, p(x) = (1/T)∫0TGD(x − c) dt and J(x) = (1/T)∫0Tċ GD(x − c) dt; the OU noise itself carries no stationary current. The teal curve is the simulated trajectory.

Rotational modes w1(x), w2(x)

The two spatial weights that define the mode plane (dots: grid points used for the projection). Each is a combination of cos/sin(kx) and cos/sin(2kx) spanning the plane of the noise-free limit cycle, normalized so that ⟨wi, wj⟩ = δij with ⟨f, g⟩ = (2/N) Σj f(xj) g(xj). They change only when the model parameters change.

Angular current

Histogram of per-step angular increments J = x1Δx2 − x2Δx1 in the mode plane. A mean shifted away from zero signals rotation; 2⟨J⟩²/Var(J) is the current-based lower bound on the asymmetry rate.

Model

dA1/dt = μ1A1 − A2 − α|A1|²A1 − β|A2|²A1,   dA2/dt = μ2A2 + εA1 − γ|A1|²A2 − δ|A2|²A2(20)
u(x, t) = Re[A1(t)eikx + A2(t)e2ikx] + ζ(x, t),   k = 2π(21)
∂tζ = −ζ + ξ,   ⟨ξ(x, t)ξ(x′, t′)⟩ = 2D δ(x − x′) δ(t − t′)(22)

Paper values: μ1 = μ2 = 0.1, α = β = γ = δ = ε = 1. Domain x ∈ [0, 2) on 41 grid points, time step Δt = 0.1; amplitudes integrated with RK4 as in the analysis code; the noise field is advanced with the exact Ornstein–Uhlenbeck update, so its stationary variance is exactly D/Δx per grid point. The noise enters only the observed field, so the amplitudes follow the same limit cycle (radius √μ for the paper values) at every D; in the mode plane the noise has standard deviation √D per axis.
